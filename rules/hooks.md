# Chispa DE · Hooks

The first line or second of every German post, story and reel: the part that decides whether someone stops scrolling. Built 2026-10-29 from Chispa's [hooks library](../../../Hooks/library.md) and the German [research](../Research/report.md), for @chispastudio.de and for German clients.

**Every German line waits for the partner's native check.** Mark a hook ✓ here once he has read it.

Four parts: the library hooks in German (H01 to H11), hooks that only work in Germany (DE01 to DE16), calls to action (C01 to C06), and reel formats (F01, F02, FD01 to FD05). The index at the bottom groups them by type, and names the best first hooks.

## Rules for every German hook

- **It pays off within a line or two.** An open hook promises an answer; the next line gives it. A hook without a payoff is clickbait and costs trust, and German viewers punish that faster than most.
- **du, singular.** One reader, warm and plain. Sie only in offers and invoices, never in a hook.
- **No promised results.** No „mehr Kunden“, „mehr Reichweite“, „garantiert“, „viral“, no promised hire or skin result. The hook names a pain or a fact; it never promises an outcome.
- **No invented numbers.** A number appears only when it is true: our prices, the package contents, a published market figure with its source in the [research](../Research/report.md), or the viewer's own sum („eine Stunde pro Post, mal 20“).
- **No named competitor.** Agencies are „viele Agenturen“ or „ein Agentur-Abo“, never a name (§ 6 UWG, comparative advertising). Every comparison must be true of the market as the research found it.
- **On screen:** no dashes and no colons; commas and periods only. A quoted phrase uses German quotes „…“. The „…“ of an open hook is fine on screen.
- **Frame 0 shows the hook,** before the voice starts. Many reels are watched on mute: the hook works as text alone.
- **AI label at first exposure.** A realistic AI picture, AI footage or AI voice carries „Mit KI erstellt“ from frame 0 (EU AI Act Art. 50). A hook may never depend on hiding that.
- **In a voice script** (ElevenLabs, `Voice/eleven.py --lang de`): each line starts with `[excited]` or `[energetic]` plus its own emotion tag and ends with „!“. An open hook becomes a full sentence. Chispa DE's own reels end on „Content, der den Daumen stoppt. Chispa!“; client reels don't.
- **No AI tells:** no „Es ist nicht nur X, es ist Y“, no three negations in a row („Kein Stress, kein Aufwand, keine Kosten“), no self-answered question („Das Geheimnis? Ganz einfach.“), no „das nächste Level“, „der ultimative Guide“, „Game Changer“, no „Was meint ihr? 👇“ unless the answer is really wanted.

Placeholders: `[Branche]` the client's trade, `[Kunden]` its customers, `[Tätigkeit]` an action, `[Zustand]` a situation the viewer is stuck in.

Niche lines are written for the first German niches (README, Decided): **Barbier** (barbershops), **Kosmetik** (Kosmetikstudios: cosmetics, never medicine, no before and after for injectables, HWG), **Handwerk** (recruiting Azubis and Fachkräfte, never a promised hire).

## Part 2 · Hooks that only work in Germany

Built on what the research found: German owners are used to agency contracts with minimum terms, „Preis auf Anfrage“, agency jargon, the Fachkräftemangel, bureaucracy jokes, distrust of hidden AI, and a split between du and Sie. The humour is dry and aimed at agency habits and at the situation, **never at the viewer or the client**.

### DE01 · The cancellation period · Die Kündigungsfrist
- **German:** „Kündigungsfrist. Drei Monate zum Quartalsende. Kennst du?“ · „Wann hast du das letzte Mal einen Vertrag gekündigt? Eben.“
- **Type:** recognition. Every German owner has fought a contract (phone, gym, agency).
- **Why it works:** the pain is felt, not explained. It sets up Chispa's one real difference, pay once, without naming anyone.
- **Payoff:** „Bei uns gibt es nichts zu kündigen. Einmal zahlen. Kein Abo.“ Post 3 is this hook.
- **For a client:** a gym or studio with no minimum term *(only if true)*: „Kündigungsfrist? Bei uns nicht.“
- **Watch out:** small-business agency packages really do run 3 to 12 months and some renew for 12 (research, prices section). Say „viele Agenturen“, never a name.

### DE02 · Price on request · Preis auf Anfrage
- **German:** „„Preis auf Anfrage.“ Warum eigentlich?“ · „Drei Wörter, bei denen jeder weiterscrollt.“
- **Type:** recognition plus a small call-out of a market habit.
- **Why it works:** German buyers want the number; the research found „auf Anfrage“ itself called a red flag.
- **Payoff:** the prices, right there. „Bei uns steht der Preis dran. 349 €, 699 €, 1.399 €. Einmal.“ Post 9 is this hook.
- **For a client:** any trade with fixed prices: „Preis auf Anfrage? Bei uns steht er im Schaufenster.“ *(only if they show prices)*

### DE03 · Do the maths · Rechne mal mit
- **German:** „Rechne mal kurz mit.“ · „Eine Stunde pro Post. Mal 20 Posts. Das sind 20 Feierabende.“
- **Type:** the viewer's own sum. Germans trust a calculation more than a claim.
- **Why it works:** the number is theirs, not ours, so nobody can call it invented. It makes time visible.
- **Payoff:** what the same month looks like done for them, in wenigen Werktagen.
- **For a client:** Handwerk: „Rechne mal mit. Eine offene Stelle, sechs Monate lang.“ *(the cost only if the client knows it)*
- **Watch out:** only sums the viewer can check in their head. „Eine Stunde pro Post“ is an example („sagen wir“), never a statistic.

### DE04 · Closing time · Feierabend
- **German:** „21 Uhr. Feierabend. Und morgen soll ein Post raus.“ · „Sonntagabend. Die Woche hat noch keinen einzigen Post.“
- **Type:** a scene the viewer has lived. Time and place in two words, then the problem.
- **Why it works:** the research's main finding: owners have the account but not the time to feed it (46% of retailers with a profile find regular posting hard, Bitkom).
- **Payoff:** a week that is already planned and finished.
- **For a client:** Barbier: „20 Uhr. Letzter Kunde raus. Und jetzt noch filmen?“

### DE05 · When it gets quieter · Wenn's ruhiger wird
- **German:** „„Wenn's ruhiger wird.“ Es wird nicht ruhiger.“ · „„Ab Januar mach ich das.“ Sagt jeder. Jedes Jahr.“ · „„Nach den Feiertagen.“ Welche Feiertage genau?“
- **Type:** the German twin of H12. The viewer hears their own excuse.
- **Why it works:** everyone has said it; it passes the send test („Schick das jemandem, der das auch immer sagt“).
- **Payoff:** a tip that helps now, not a sale. One thing to post this week.
- **Watch out:** **as advice, never as pleading for clients.** The founder rejected the Israeli version on Chispa's own feed as begging (2026-10-26); on @chispastudio.de it carries a real tip, for clients it fits as is. Timely only around a real season (Advent, January, after the summer).

### DE06 · POV
- **German:** „POV. Du bist Barbier, hast heute 14 Bärte gemacht, und Instagram will einen Post.“ · „POV. Du hast eine Stellenanzeige geschrieben. Es ist Dezember. Keiner hat sich gemeldet.“
- **Type:** a first-person scene, a native Instagram form that German Gen Z and Millennials read without explanation.
- **Why it works:** the viewer is cast in the scene in two words.
- **Payoff:** the scene turns; the post is already done.
- **Watch out:** „POV“ then a period, never a colon. The scene is an example, so its numbers are scenery, not claims.

### DE07 · Buzzword bingo · Buzzword-Bingo
- **German:** „Synergien. Funnel. Touchpoints. Oder einfach ein guter Post?“ · „Strategie-Call, Kick-off, Onboarding. Wir fangen einfach an.“
- **Type:** dry humour at agency jargon, which German KMU agencies use a lot (research, branding section).
- **Why it works:** the viewer has sat through that call; laughing at it together builds trust.
- **Payoff:** how plain it is with us: three steps over WhatsApp. Post 12 is this hook; FD03 is the reel.

### DE08 · Always done it this way · Haben wir schon immer so gemacht
- **German:** „„Haben wir schon immer so gemacht.“ Anzeige in der Zeitung, und dann warten.“
- **Type:** a famous German sentence, turned gently.
- **Why it works:** every Handwerk owner knows it, and most have said it. Self-irony, not mockery.
- **Payoff:** where the next Azubi actually is (Instagram reaches 40% of people 14+ weekly, more than any other network, ARD/ZDF 2025).
- **For a client:** Handwerk recruiting, its strongest opener.
- **Watch out:** respect for the trade. The joke is the habit, never the owner.

### DE09 · Unpopular opinion · Unpopuläre Meinung
- **German:** „Unpopuläre Meinung. Für guten Content brauchst du kein Abo.“ · „Unpopuläre Meinung. Nicht jedes Geschäft braucht TikTok.“
- **Type:** a clear opinion. It invites agreement and argument, both good for reach.
- **Why it works:** a stated position stands out among neutral feeds.
- **Payoff:** the reasoning, briefly, and true.
- **Watch out:** only opinions Chispa really holds and can defend in the comments.

### DE10 · Your next apprentice · Dein nächster Azubi
- **German:** „Dein nächster Azubi scrollt gerade.“ · „Fachkräftemangel? Deine Bewerber sind auf Instagram, nicht in der Zeitung.“
- **Type:** recruiting, for Handwerk (the second German niche).
- **Why it works:** 16,000+ training places stayed unfilled in 2025 (DHZ); the owner feels it every day.
- **Payoff:** what a recruiting reel shows: the real workshop, the team, a day at work. Post 8 and post 14 are this hook.
- **Watch out:** **never promise a hire.** „Zeig, wie es bei dir ist“, never „so findest du deinen Azubi“.

### DE11 · In triplicate · In dreifacher Ausfertigung
- **German:** „Antrag auf einen Instagram-Post. Bitte in dreifacher Ausfertigung.“ · „Formular 7b. Social Media. Bearbeitungszeit unbekannt.“
- **Type:** bureaucracy humour, a shared German joke.
- **Why it works:** instant recognition, no explanation needed, very shareable.
- **Payoff:** with us it is three WhatsApp messages and in wenigen Werktagen.
- **Watch out:** joke about paperwork in general, never about a real office or the Finanzamt by name.

### DE12 · The Monday plan · Ab Montag
- **German:** „„Ab Montag poste ich regelmäßig.“ Jeden Montag.“
- **Type:** recognition of a good intention that never lasts.
- **Why it works:** the viewer laughs at themselves; it is honest without shaming.
- **Payoff:** a plan that does not depend on Monday's motivation: a finished batch with a posting plan.

### DE13 · Says so on the label · Steht drauf
- **German:** „Mit KI erstellt. Steht bei uns drauf.“ · „Wir sagen dir, was KI gemacht hat. Und was wir.“
- **Type:** transparency turned into trust.
- **Why it works:** 35% of Germans say AI ads would hurt their trust, 77% want them labelled, 60% trust more with transparency (research). The label is what most fear to show; showing it first wins.
- **Payoff:** how it works, AI does the drafts, people check every piece. Post 16 is this hook.
- **Watch out:** never lead a sales post with AI; this is a trust post. Speed, a fixed price and finished quality are the pitch.

### DE14 · Say it like this · Sprich Tschis-pa
- **German:** „Chispa. Sprich Tschis-pa. Spanisch für Funke.“
- **Type:** the name story. For Chispa DE's own feed only.
- **Why it works:** Germans may read it as „Kispa“; a one-time cue removes the doubt and gives the brand its meaning.
- **Payoff:** „Jedes Geschäft hat einen Funken. Wir finden deinen.“ Post 1 carries it.

### DE15 · Quick question · Kurze Frage
- **German:** „Kurze Frage. Wann war dein letzter Post?“
- **Type:** a direct question the viewer answers in their head.
- **Why it works:** the answer is usually „zu lange her“, and the viewer stays for what that means.
- **Payoff:** what a new customer sees when the last post is from March, and one easy fix.
- **Watch out:** a real question, not a self-answered one; the next line is information, not „Genau!“.

### DE16 · Arabic, in Germany · Auf Arabisch
- **German and Arabic:** „Du hast einen Salon in Deutschland und sprichst Arabisch?“ with the Arabic line under it, written by the partner in his own dialect.
- **Type:** a direct call to the niche where Chispa DE is nearly alone: about a third of German founders have an immigration history, and no Arabic social media agency for local shops turned up in Germany (research, niches section).
- **Why it works:** the viewer feels addressed in their own language, often for the first time by a German studio.
- **Payoff:** „Wir sprechen Arabisch. Deine Posts kommen auf Deutsch, gern auch zweisprachig.“ Post 15 is this hook.
- **Watch out:** the Arabic is the partner's, never machine-written. Bilingual content is offered as a test, not promised as proven demand. Turkish-owned businesses get German like everyone else.

## Part 3 · Calls to action

**All sales are inbound** (§ 7 UWG): we write only to people who wrote first or asked. Every call to action below asks the viewer to act; none leads to us messaging someone who didn't.

### C01 · Comment a word · Kommentar-Wort
- **German:** „Schreib „Funke“ in die Kommentare, und wir schicken dir 10 Post-Ideen für dein Geschäft.“
- **What it needs:** a real thing to send (a one-page PDF), sent by hand or by an automatic reply. Send exactly what was promised and nothing else unasked: no follow-up sales message after it.
- **Watch out:** an automatic DM tool (ManyChat or similar) processes personal data: check it with the privacy policy first.

### C02 · The WhatsApp word · Schreib uns „Paket“
- **German:** „Schreib uns „Paket“ auf WhatsApp. Link in der Bio.“
- **Why it works:** the research's recommended close: one word, on the channel 85% of Germans use; the person writes first, so the chat is lawful.

### C03 · Save it · Speichern
- **German:** „Speicher dir das für Sonntagabend.“ · „Speichern, bevor du deinen nächsten Post planst.“
- **For:** lists, tips and carousels. Saves signal value to Instagram.

### C04 · Send it · Schick das
- **German:** „Schick das deinem Barbier.“ · „Schick das jemandem, der auch „ab Januar“ sagt.“
- **For:** reach. Sends are the strongest signal (Playbook [reach.md](../../../Playbook/reach.md)). The viewer shares it themselves; we never ask for contacts.

### C05 · Link in bio · Link in der Bio
- **German:** „Preise und Pakete, Link in der Bio.“
- **For:** offer posts and stories. The bio link also leads to the Impressum.

### C06 · Soft · Einfach schreiben
- **German:** „Fragen? Schreib uns einfach.“ · „Erzähl uns von deinem Geschäft, gern als Sprachnachricht.“
- **For:** the end of anything, the gentlest ask. Never „Jetzt zuschlagen“, never „Nur heute“ or „Nur noch 3 Plätze“ unless it is true.

## Part 4 · Reel formats

### F01 · The list reel, German
- **The reel:** „5 Posts, die jedes Geschäft braucht.“ One item per screen, about two seconds each, numbered „01“ to „05“, each word landing as it is spoken, closed by C01 or C03.
- **The visual pun:** the spark, as in Israel. For Handwerk clients, a spirit level whose bubble lands in the middle on every item.

### F02 · About to happen, German
- **The reel:** a countdown to a moment that never lands, then the turn: „Sieben Sekunden hast du hingeschaut.“ The shape and its rules are in the [library](../../../Hooks/library.md#f02--about-to-happen).
- **German moments:** a full mug on the Werkbank tipping toward the open plan · a clipper sliding off the barber's counter toward the sink · a finger over „Für alle löschen“ in a chat with a customer (it taps „Senden“ instead, and the Chispa package arrives) · a Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte sliding off the tray.
- **Germany changes one thing:** the realistic footage carries „Mit KI erstellt“ from frame 0, so the twist „and it isn't even real“ is gone. The turn is the viewer's own seven seconds, which still works: „Sieben Sekunden. Jetzt stell dir vor, das ist dein Reel.“

### FD01 · The bill · Die Rechnung
- **What it is:** a receipt prints line by line, each line one habit of an agency subscription: „Mindestlaufzeit 12 Monate“, „Einrichtungsgebühr“, „Strategie-Call“, „Kündigungsfrist 3 Monate“. It keeps printing past the frame. Cut: a short receipt, one line, „Chispa. 20 Posts, 20 Stories, 3 Reels. Einmal. 699 €.“
- **Why it works:** a German instinct, read the bill. The contrast is the offer itself, shown as an object.
- **Watch out:** every line on the long receipt must be a real, common term (the research lists them), with no prices on it unless sourced, and no agency named.

### FD02 · The small print · Das Kleingedruckte
- **What it is:** a thumb scrolls a contract that never ends, legal German shrinking smaller and smaller, „automatische Verlängerung“ highlighted. The thumb stops; the whole Chispa offer fits on one line: „Einmal zahlen. Kein Abo.“
- **Why it works:** no voice needed, works on mute, and the payoff is the brand line.

### FD03 · Buzzword bingo · Buzzword-Bingo
- **What it is:** a bingo card; each buzzword the voice says gets crossed out („Synergien“, „Funnel“, „Touchpoints“, „Mindset“, „Content-Strategie-Workshop“). Bingo. Then the card flips: „Oder einfach Content.“ and three finished posts land on it.
- **Hook:** DE07. **Why it works:** dry humour at the market, which the research names as the German register that works.

### FD04 · POV, closing time · POV Feierabend
- **What it is:** a day of a barber or Handwerk owner in fast real cuts (the client's own footage, or Wan footage labelled „Mit KI erstellt“), the clock in the corner running to 21:00, then the phone and the empty draft. Turn: the week's posts are already planned.
- **Hook:** DE04 or DE06. **Why it works:** the viewer recognises their own day before a word is said.

### FD05 · Unboxing the package · Paket auspacken
- **What it is:** a box opens; 20 posts fan out like cards, 20 stories stack up, three reels play side by side. „Das steckt in 699 €.“
- **Why it works:** the research says show the product, not the team: very few German agencies ever show a finished package.
- **Watch out:** show real demo work (tagged „Beispiel“), never a real client's posts without consent.
